Steve, here's your plan to learn about trains and how to fix them:

**Study:** Start with the basics—physics, electrical systems, hydraulics, and diesel mechanics. Choose a focus (freight, commuter, steam, etc.) and get hands-on training at a community college, trade school, or apprenticeship. Use resources like community college Railroad Technology programs, FRA.gov, and books like "The Railroad: What It Is, What It Does." Respect safety rules and consider volunteering at a rail museum.

**Career:** Pursue roles like Locomotive Mechanic, Railcar Technician, or Diesel Mechanic. Steps: get a high school diploma/GED, complete a 1–2 year technical program (diesel/heavy equipment), apply for entry-level shop or yard jobs, earn certifications (ASE, OSHA, welding), and join a union apprenticeship. Look for apprenticeships with major freight railroads or transit agencies.

Short version: Get technical training, get your foot in the door with an entry-level rail job, and work your way up through apprenticeships and certifications.
